Fraud detection on big data: batch or streaming?

Big Data Computing mini project. The same synthetic transactions and the same rules run through Hadoop + Hive (batch) and Kafka (streaming), so any difference comes from the architecture alone.

Full runs in progress. Results appear in the Results section once the metrics files are loaded.

Shared rules

Both pipelines read the same config.json.

High amount

Amount above the user's mean plus 3 standard deviations. Streaming needs 10 past transactions first.

Rapid burst

More than 3 transactions by one user inside 120 seconds.

Impossible travel

More than 500 km from the previous transaction, within 30 minutes of it.

Odd hour

Between 00:00 and 05:00 with an amount of at least 1,500.

The data

Synthetic and reproducible

Fixed seed. About 50 transactions per user over 14 days, with home cities, favourite merchants and personal spend levels.

Labelled fraud

1.5% of rows: high amount 40%, burst 25%, travel 25%, odd hour 10%.

Deliberately hard

A quarter of each pattern sits just under the thresholds, so recall cannot be a trivial 100%.

Three sizes

10K, 100K and 1M rows, to test how each approach scales.

How we compare them

Batch (Hadoop + Hive)Streaming (Kafka)
Speed measured asEnd-to-end job time, from HDFS load to flags collectedPer-event latency: p50, p95, p99, max
What it seesThe whole history, past and futureOnly events that have already arrived
Expected weak spotFlags arrive only when the job endsCold start: new users have no baseline
Load test10K, 100K, 1M rowsReplay rates up to 50K events/sec, tracking consumer lag
Also measuredPrecision, recall, F1, false-positive rate, per-fraud-type recall, CPU and memory
